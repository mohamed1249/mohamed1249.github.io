  • The introduction is basically setting up the book’s main complaint:
    • The Data Science Industrial Complex = the whole ecosystem of hype, tools, job titles, consultants, products, and expectations built around data.
    • The problem is not just bad models. It’s misalignment:
      • businesses expect magic, managers hire the wrong people, data scientists enter companies that aren’t ready, executives hear jargon, projects die, money burns.
    • The terminology changes — Big Data→ Data Science ↔ ML ↔ Deep Learning ↔ AI — but many underlying problems are old problems wearing new clothes.
    • The book reduces most failures to three causes:
      • Hard problems
      • Weak critical thinking
      • Poor communication
  • The first one is important because it kills the fantasy that enough data + enough compute = certainty.
    • Even with excellent analysts and good data: models can still be wrong because reality itself is messy.
  • And the communication point is one of the strongest themes of the whole book: Data scientists and decision-makers often speak different languages.
    • That’s not a soft-skill side issue. It’s one of the reasons technically correct work still fails.
  • These two are worth keeping almost verbatim:
    • Informed decisions require data.
    • Predictions should never be 100% confident. a prediction is an estimate under uncertainty, not a prophecy.
